Planet earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known astronomical object to harbor life.

Layers of the earth

The Four Main Layers

  • Crust: The outermost, thinnest rocky shell where we live. Its thickness ranges from about 5 km under the oceans (oceanic crust) to 30–70 km under continents (continental crust). It is made mostly of silicate rocks like granite and basalt.
  • Mantle: A massive layer of dense, hot, semi-solid rock extending down to a depth of 2,890 km. It makes up about 84% of Earth's total volume. Though solid over short timeframes, it slowly flows over millions of years due to extreme heat and pressure (convection currents).
  • Outer Core: A liquid layer roughly 2,300 km thick, composed primarily of iron and nickel. Temperatures range from 4,000°C to 5,000°C. The movement of this churning liquid metal creates electric currents, generating Earth's magnetic field.
  • Inner Core: The innermost sphere with a radius of about 1,220 km. Despite being hotter than the surface of the Sun (around 5,400°C), it is completely solid due to the crushing pressure of the entire planet pressing down on it. It is made of solid iron and nickel.

The Astronomical Object

  1. Composition: It consists of four distinct layers: a solid iron-nickel inner core, a liquid outer core, a thick mantle of molten rock, and a rocky outer crust.
  2. Water World: Oceans of liquid water cover approximately 70.8% of the surface, making it unique among rocky planets in our solar system.
  3. Atmosphere: A rich, oxygenated atmosphere and an ozone layer shield the surface from harmful solar radiation, allowing a diverse biosphere to thrive.
